01 — Algorithmic Bias

When data inherits
history's prejudice

AI systems learn from human-generated data — and human data is laden with centuries of structural inequality. From hiring algorithms to criminal sentencing tools, bias compounds at scale in ways that are often invisible until examined closely.

02 — Ethical Frameworks

The principles
AI must uphold

01
Transparency & Explainability

Users have a right to understand how automated decisions are made. Black-box models — especially in high-stakes settings like credit, healthcare, and criminal justice — undermine informed consent and accountability.

Governance
02
Fairness & Non-Discrimination

AI should not produce or reinforce discrimination based on race, gender, age, disability, or other protected characteristics. Fairness is not one metric — it is a constellation of competing definitions that require deliberate trade-offs.

Social Justice
03
Privacy & Data Sovereignty

Training on personal data without meaningful consent creates extractive relationships between platforms and individuals. Privacy-preserving techniques like federated learning and differential privacy offer partial but imperfect remedies.

Rights
04
Human Oversight & Control

As AI capabilities expand, meaningful human oversight becomes both more important and more difficult to ensure. HITL (Human-in-the-Loop) design is often nominal — rubber-stamping automation rather than genuine review.

Safety
05
Accountability & Redress

When AI causes harm, who is responsible? Diffuse accountability across developers, deployers, and regulators creates gaps where no one is answerable. Meaningful redress mechanisms are essential.

Governance
06
Environmental Impact

Training large models consumes enormous energy. GPT-3 training emitted roughly the CO₂ of five trans-American flights. As models scale, the environmental cost of AI is an underexamined ethical dimension.

03 — Regulation & Policy

The global race to govern AI

2016
United States
NIST AI Risk Management Framework

The National Institute of Standards and Technology released foundational guidelines for AI risk, establishing vocabulary and voluntary practices for trustworthy AI system design — setting a precedent for risk-based thinking.

2019
OECD / G20
OECD AI Principles Adopted

42 countries endorsed five principles for trustworthy AI: inclusive growth, human-centred values, transparency, robustness, and accountability. The first intergovernmental standard on AI — soft law, but widely influential.

2021
European Union
EU AI Act Proposal

The European Commission proposed a risk-tiered regulatory framework: prohibited uses (social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance), high-risk systems requiring conformity assessment, and lighter-touch rules for general-purpose AI.

2023
United States
Executive Order on AI Safety

Biden's EO required safety evaluations before releasing powerful AI models, mandated watermarking of AI-generated content, and directed federal agencies to develop AI governance plans — the most comprehensive U.S. federal AI action to date.

2024
European Union
EU AI Act Enacted

The world's first comprehensive AI law passed into force, with phased implementation through 2027. It bans certain uses, imposes strict requirements on high-risk systems, and requires GPAI model transparency — setting a global benchmark.

2025
Global
Fragmented Regulatory Landscape

China enacted sector-specific AI rules. The UK pursued a principles-based approach. G7 nations signed the Hiroshima Code of Conduct for AI developers. Regulatory divergence between jurisdictions remains a central challenge for global AI governance.

04 — AI in Healthcare

Healing —
or widening
the gap?

AI holds remarkable promise in medicine: earlier cancer detection, drug discovery acceleration, and reduced diagnostic error. But it also risks encoding healthcare inequality at scale, replacing the art of medicine with opaque algorithmic recommendations.

"Medicine is a social science, and politics is nothing else but medicine on a large scale."

— Rudolf Virchow, 1848
Dermatology AI & Skin Tone Bias
Risk

The majority of skin disease datasets are over 80% lighter skin tones. AI dermatology tools trained on these datasets show significantly lower diagnostic accuracy for darker skin — widening disparities in melanoma detection for populations already underserved by dermatology.

Sepsis Prediction & Algorithmic Opacity
Alert

Several hospitals deployed proprietary sepsis prediction algorithms that flagged patients for early intervention. Independent audits of one widely-used system (Epic Sepsis Model) found it missed two-thirds of sepsis cases — yet clinicians trusted model outputs over their own judgment.

AlphaFold & Drug Discovery
Breakthrough

DeepMind's AlphaFold predicted the structure of virtually every known protein — 200 million structures — in months, a task that would have taken billions of research-years by hand. It has accelerated drug development for malaria, antibiotic resistance, and rare diseases.

Optum's Race-Biased Risk Score
Documented

A widely used commercial algorithm for identifying high-risk patients used healthcare cost as a proxy for health need — but Black patients with equal levels of illness generated lower costs due to unequal healthcare access, causing systematic under-referral to care management.

AI-Assisted Radiology
Promise

FDA-cleared AI tools for chest X-ray and mammography triage have shown sensitivity comparable to radiologists, enabling earlier detection and reducing reading backlogs — particularly valuable in low-resource settings without specialist access.
